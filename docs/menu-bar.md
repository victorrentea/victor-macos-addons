# Menu Bar & Menu (MenuBarManager)

What stays a menu row and why; rows removed in favour of the ⌘⌃ cheat-sheet (see `keyboard-overlays.md`).

- **☠️ Kill port** — submenu with recent ports + custom port dialog

**Shortcuts/menu (2026-06):** The 🔥 menu item is **Whip Agent** (briefly just "Whip"; formerly "Whip Claude"), bound to **⌃W** globally (event tap, suppressed) and shown as ⌃W in the menu. NB ⌃W globally shadows the usual "delete word backwards" in terminals/editors. The bottom-left notification pill (`BottomLeftBanner`) renders the hover-hint chip ("Hover to undo/snooze/continue/Send") **pinned to the pill's bottom-left corner** (fixed — no longer riding/moving), with the **orange countdown bar filling the region to the right of the chip** (from the chip's right edge to the pill's right edge) left→right. Countdown banners are floored at **30% of the screen width** (`countdownMinWidthFraction`) so the fixed chip and the bar to its right always fit; plain (non-countdown) banners still hug their text. Reusable across every banner caller.

**Menu (2026-08-14) — the menu is not a second cheat-sheet.** Holding ⌘⌃ draws every one of this app's ⌘⌃ shortcuts on the secondary screen at once (`KeymapOverlay`), which is faster than opening a menu and walking a submenu — so rows whose only content was "this key exists" were removed: **📕 Catalog** (⌘⌃K), **📧 Gmail** (⌘⌃G), **📅 Calendar** (⌘⌃L), **Tile Terminals** (⌘⌃A), **Terminal in workspace** (⌘⌃T), **🎅 workspace (plain)** (F8) and the two greyed-out reminders (⌘⌃V *Paste Emotions*, ⌘⌃S *Selection to Notes*). Every one of those keys is still bound in `EventTapManager` — only the rows are gone, and `MenuBarManager.onOpenCatalog` / `onOpenGmail` / `onOpenCalendar` survive as the tap's landing points. **🔁 Restart** went too (reopening the app relaunches it). **`🎅 workspace` was broken**, not redundant: it opened claude in `~/workspace/ai`, a folder that became `skills-private` and no longer exists. What the Extra submenu keeps is what no key replaces: **📝 Paste Clipboard to Notes** (a menu click cannot capture the previous app's selection, so ⌘⌃S has no menu form at all), the **Emoji Overlay**, **Dark Mode** and **🟡 Cursor Glow** toggles, and the two per-repo claude launchers (each opens claude in a named repo, renamed, in its own screen quarter).

**🟡 Cursor Glow** (`CursorGlow.swift`, 2026-09-04) — macOS has no API to re-skin the system pointer bitmap itself, so this fakes a glow: a click-through, all-spaces `NSPanel` chases `NSEvent.mouseLocation` every 30ms (the same trick `BusyCursorSpinner` uses for its one-shot busy indicator) and paints a soft yellow radial gradient centered exactly on the hotspot, showing through under whatever cursor shape is currently drawn. State persists across restarts (`CursorGlowSettings.isEnabled` in `UserDefaults`); `AppDelegate` starts it on launch if it was left on. The 🔥 item reads **Whip Agent**, and **📸 Screenshot (hold to crop)** is one row teaching both halves of ⌃P.

**⏱️ Resumed — the row that colours (2026-08-14).** "⏱️ Resumed 1h 51m ago" turns **yellow past 1h15m** and **red past 1h45m** (`RHTimerMonitor.urgency`, pure + unit-tested; thresholds are Victor's teaching rhythm — a section runs about an hour and a quarter, and past an hour and three quarters the room is gone). The row is read at a glance mid-sentence, so the colour has to carry it: by the time you have *read* "1h 52m" you have spent the attention the glance was meant to save. It is drawn through `attributedTitle` because the item is **disabled** — a readout, nothing to click — and AppKit dims a disabled item's plain title, which is the opposite of what a warning wants; an explicit foreground colour survives that dimming. The yellow is appearance-resolved like the menu-bar apple glyph (`systemYellow` reads on a dark menu and vanishes on a light one, so light mode gets a dark amber). The ⏱️ moved into `RHTimerMonitor.formatElapsed` itself, so the row no longer changes shape the moment it starts saying something.

## 📤 Mail clipboard to myself

Sits **directly under 🔳 Display clipboard link**, because the two rows answer the same question — *what is on the clipboard* — and differ only in where it goes: one puts it on the wall for the room, this one mails it to Victor as a `Reminder` (⌘⌃P, `ReminderMail`; the mechanism is in [hotkeys-launchers.md](hotkeys-launchers.md)).

It breaks *the menu is not a second cheat-sheet* knowingly, and not on the 🔴 raw-audio row's terms — there is no invisible state here to reveal. The justification is the other one: **this key SENDS**, into a real inbox, with nothing to confirm and nothing to undo. Every removed row was a shortcut for something reversible; a menu row is the deliberate, aimed form of an irreversible gesture — you read the title before you click it, which is not true of a key you hit mid-sentence.

The row carries `⌘⌃P` as a real `keyEquivalent`, not as text in the title, and it **cannot fire the send twice**: the event tap returns `nil` for ⌘⌃P, so the keystroke never reaches AppKit and the menu equivalent never matches. That makes the equivalent a fallback as well as a label — with no Accessibility grant the tap is not running, and the menu is then the only way to fire it at all.

The click runs `sendClipboardReminder()` off the main thread, exactly as ⌘⌃P and `GET /test/reminder` do: the pasteboard read and any JPEG re-encode happen inline, and only the receipt banner hops back.

## 🔴 Record raw audio

A checkbox row at the **bottom of the 💬 Transcribing submenu**, under a separator that divides it from the mic-source rows above: those choose *which* mic is heard, this one decides whether that mic is also written to disk. It used to be a top-level row and breaks the "the menu is not a second cheat-sheet" rule on purpose, and the justification is narrow: while it is on, the microphone is being written to disk, and **there is no other place that fact appears**. Every other row here either triggers something or repeats a shortcut; this one is the only readout of an invisible, privacy-relevant, disk-filling state — so it also carries how much is recorded (`🔴 Recording raw audio — 2.4 h`), which is the next question anyone asks. Off, it reads plainly as `Record raw audio`, with no 🔴 and no checkmark, so the on-state cannot be mistaken at a glance. Moving it inside a submenu would have destroyed exactly the visibility that justifies it, so two things compensate: while capture is on the **🔴 prefixes the parent `Transcribing` title itself** (in every state — the mic keeps being written to disk whatever transcription is doing), and the parent row now **keeps its submenu and stays enabled even when transcription is off or battery-paused**, since a disabled parent would make a running capture impossible to stop at the exact moment you most want it gone. The row is also painted from `isRecordingRaw`/`recordedRawHours` held on `MenuBarManager` rather than straight onto the item, because `AppDelegate` reports the state **before** the menu is built — writing it to a nil item silently lost a capture that was already armed at launch. Details and the file format are in [transcription.md](transcription.md).

## 🔄 Reverse Mouse Wheel

A checkbox in the **👩🏻‍💻 Extra** submenu, beside 🟡 Cursor Glow, and the **only row in the whole menu whose default is on**. It breaks *the menu is not a second cheat-sheet* on the 🔴 raw-audio row's terms rather than despite them: there is no key to teach — nothing is bound — and no other place in the app where the state appears. When a scroll goes the wrong way the first question is "is this app doing that?", and this row is the only thing that can answer it.

The default is the point, and it is the one thing that could have shipped wrong silently. The row replaces **Scroll Reverser**, a utility that was *already running* when it was uninstalled (2026-09-07), so a first launch with nothing stored has to reproduce the machine as it was — and `UserDefaults.bool(forKey:)` answers **false** for a key nobody has written, which is exactly how a replacement like this ends up installed in the off position with no error anywhere. `ScrollReversalSettings.isEnabled` is therefore `object(forKey:) as? Bool ?? true`, and there is a test that removes the key and asserts on it.

**Nothing is notified when it is clicked**, unlike every other toggle here. The event tap reads `ScrollReversalSettings` itself, on the scroll event it is already holding, so there is no cached mirror and no window in which the tick and the wheel disagree — the failure mode a "push the new value to the component" toggle has by construction. It costs a `UserDefaults` lookup per scroll event, measured at 630 ns (200k reads, `-O`) against a tap whose watchdog is measured in seconds. The mechanism, the trackpad exclusion and why the reversal sits *ahead* of the Cmd+scroll font zoom are in [hotkeys-launchers.md](hotkeys-launchers.md).

## 💬📺 Live Subtitles

A **ticked** row, sitting with 📸 Screenshot rather than among the toggles in Extra, and carrying its ⌘⌃U in the key-equivalent column. It breaks "the menu is not a second cheat-sheet" for the same reason 🔴 raw-audio recording does, and the reason is worth stating because the row was left out at first on exactly that rule: the rule holds for a row that only **repeats a key**, and this one carries **state** — on the built-in Retina, which is the screen the room is looking at. A tick answers "is the room being subtitled right now?" without pressing anything to find out, and pressing something to find out is precisely what you cannot do mid-session. **The tick follows the band, not the click** (`setLiveCaptions`, called from `LiveCaptions.onStateChanged`): the key, this row and `GET /test/captions` all end up in the same place, and none of them can leave the row claiming the room is being subtitled when it is not.
