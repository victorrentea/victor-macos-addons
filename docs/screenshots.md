# Screenshots (⌃P)

- **📸 Screenshot (⌃P) — clipboard AND disk, always both** — captures the display under the cursor, puts it on the pasteboard (PNG + TIFF, so both the clip-stack poller and a CLI can read it) **and** files it as `~/Library/Caches/ro.victorrentea.macos-addons/screenshots/YYYY-MM-DD_HH-mm-ss.jpg`. There used to be two shortcuts — ⌃P to the clipboard, ⌃⇧P to the session folder — and choosing between them is a decision nobody makes correctly mid-workshop: you take the shot to paste it somewhere, and only hours later, writing the summary, discover it was worth keeping. So **⌃⇧P is gone** (VS Code gets its Command Palette back; the tap's branch now requires `!hasShift`) and there is one menu item. **The border flash is raised the instant `screencapture` exits — before the clipboard work, not after it**: re-encoding a retina capture to PNG *and* TIFF costs hundreds of milliseconds, and doing that first pushed the yellow border that far behind the keypress, which simply read as "⌃P is laggy". It cannot be raised any earlier than the capture: the flash is a real window, so anything drawn before the shot is drawn *into* the shot. **Held, ⌃P becomes the crosshair crop** (`CropSelectionOverlay`) — the tool ⌃⇧P used to reach, minus the decision: you no longer pick "full or crop" before pressing, you pick it by how long you keep the key down. **Nothing fires on the keyDown**: which of the two you meant is answered on the *release* — under `ScreenshotHoldPolicy.holdSeconds` = 0.45 s takes the screen, over it opens the crosshair (an **autorepeat** keyDown answers it earlier, so with key repeat on the crosshair appears while the key is still down; the keyUp is the fallback where repeat is off, and is the only reason `keyUp` is in the tap's event mask). The first version instead fired the full-screen shot on the keyDown, to keep a tap free of any "is this a hold?" delay — but that delay is not what waiting costs: the release costs only the length of the keypress itself (~100 ms for a tap), while firing early meant every hold took a full screen nobody asked for **and flashed a whole-screen yellow border over the crosshair already waiting for the drag**. So no shot, no border, and nothing to clean up afterwards. Esc cancels the selection, which writes no file, and nothing else runs: the clipboard and the folder are left exactly as they were. **The border a crop gets is drawn around the crop**, not around the screen (`ScreenCaptureFlash.flash(around:)`, a ring sitting entirely **inside** the region, fading inward from its edges exactly the way the screen-sized flash fades in from the screen's — so what lights up is the captured pixels themselves; drawn outside it read as a frame *around* something, which is the wrong answer to "what did I just take"): a whole-screen frame after a selection claims the wrong thing, since not taking the whole screen is what the crop was for. **The crosshair is ours, not `screencapture -i`'s** (`CropSelectionOverlay`), and one gesture is the whole reason: **⌘ held mid-drag moves the box** instead of resizing it, and moves it **only inside the screen the drag started on** — push into an edge and it stops there rather than walking off. macOS's own selection offers that on the *space bar* and cannot be told to read ⌘ instead; translating one into the other means posting a synthetic space into a subprocess that owns the screen, which the moment the modifier leaks arrives as ⌘Space — Spotlight, on the projector — and still leaves nothing to ask about staying on screen. The travel is measured from the **total** delta since ⌘ went down, never accumulated tick by tick: that is what brings the box straight back when you push it into an edge and pull away again, instead of leaving it stuck there with the overshoot remembered. Releasing ⌘ resumes resizing from wherever the box now is, and if an edge held it back the corner stays exactly that far behind the cursor rather than snapping to it — the honest record of "it could not follow you" (`CropFlashGeometry.clampedTranslation`, pure + unit-tested; the box is confined to one display for the second reason too — a capture is one display's pixels). **⌥ held mid-drag draws the box from its middle**: the point the drag started from stops being a corner and becomes the *centre*, with the mouse still on a corner, so the box grows in both directions at once — the way you frame something you are already pointing at, instead of first hunting for where its top-left corner would be. The screen is honoured **symmetrically** (`CropFlashGeometry.centeredRect`, pure + unit-tested): a half-extent that would push one edge off the display is cut on *both* sides, because clamping just the free corner — the obvious reuse of `clamped(_:within:)` — keeps the box on screen while silently sliding its middle, which is the one thing ⌥ promises not to do. It combines with ⌘: what then travels is the box ⌥ drew, and the move takes hold of the corner the **box** actually has rather than the raw mouse corner — in centred mode an edge may already be holding the half-extent back, and carrying that unused reach into the move would let the box *grow* on its way across the screen instead of merely travelling. Toggling ⌥ while ⌘ is down restarts the measurement, since ⌥ changes what the box is. Owning the selection pays for itself a second time: **the crop rectangle is now known, not guessed**. `screencapture -i` reported nothing about the region it took — the jpg carries a size and no origin — so the old code reconstructed it by sampling `CGEventSource.buttonState` every 8 ms from behind screencapture's own overlay (`CropDragTracker`, now gone), and the border then had to be **checked against the saved file's pixel dimensions** before it dared draw itself, drawing **nothing at all** when the guess didn't line up (`CropFlashGeometry.matchesCapture`, also gone). The border is simply drawn around the rectangle that was captured. What survives from the tracker is *how* the overlay is driven: a 60 Hz poll of `NSEvent.mouseLocation`, `CGEventSource.buttonState`, `NSEvent.modifierFlags` and `CGEventSource.keyState(53)` rather than AppKit events, because that needs no key window and no app activation — the crosshair never takes focus from whatever the room is looking at. Its panels do *accept* mouse events (and drop them on the floor), which is the one thing keeping the drag from selecting text in the app underneath. Since the pointer belongs to the frontmost app and not to us, the overlay **draws its own crosshair** — full-width and full-height guides under the cursor — plus a live `w × h` readout under the box and, before the drag, the line `trage o zonă · ⌘ mută · ⌥ din centru · Esc anulează`: the hold is one gesture nothing on screen reveals, and the two modifiers are the others. While a box is up the readout says which mode it is in — `⦿ centru`, `✥ mut`, or both. **Esc, a right-click, or a drag under 6 pt cancel**, which writes no file. **The capture is a whole-display shot cropped in-process**, not `screencapture -R`: that obvious tool answers `could not create image from display with rect` on macOS 15.7 for every rectangle, on every display, with and without `-D`. The extra decode buys back the scale as well — the region is cut at the pixel scale read off the capture itself (`CropFlashGeometry.pixelCrop`, which makes the Cocoa-y-up → image-y-down flip once, pure + unit-tested) instead of trusting `backingScaleFactor`. The panels come down and the compositor is given 100 ms to forget them before the shutter, or the dimming would be in the picture. Two traps found by driving the whole thing with synthesized drags, both of which outlived the keyDown-capture design: two captures can be **named in the same second** and the second then overwrote the first, so every capture goes through `ScreenshotManager.uniqueURL`, which appends `-2` after the timestamp the summarizer skills parse; and a border of ours raised while a selection is up gets dragged into the crop and photographed by it — worse under `screencapture -i`, where our panel landed above the system's own selection overlay and runs were observed ending with the selection cancelled and no file written — so a crop **suppresses** screen-sized flashes for its whole duration (`beginSuppression`/`endSuppression`) rather than only cancelling the ones already on screen. The menu carries **one** item, **📸 Screenshot (hold to crop)**: clicking it takes the whole screen (a click cannot be held) and the title is where the other half of the shortcut is taught, since a hold is the one gesture nothing on screen reveals. A second row for the crop would have been one you can never usefully click, explaining a key you already have. The file is the capture and the clipboard is a **copy of that file**, not a second `screencapture -c` run: two runs are two different moments, and the picture you pasted has to be the picture the summary can quote. **Caches, not `/tmp`, because these files must be reclaimable**: Caches is the one folder emptying the Trash, Storage Management and every cleaner tool actually reach, and macOS may purge it under disk pressure — all welcome, because the folder is a staging area, never an archive. It also bounds itself (`ScreenshotRetentionPolicy`, pure + unit-tested, pruned off the capture path after every shot): older than **14 days** or past a **2 GB** total and the oldest go, except the **newest file, which is never deleted** — that is the guard against a wrong clock throwing away the capture taken one second ago, the one about to be pasted. **The cursor's position rides in the filename** — `2026-08-14_00-34-42_at1200x500.jpg`, global CG points (y down from the primary's top, so a screen to its left gives negatives), sampled *before* the shutter since that is what `-C` draws into the picture. The shot shows the pointer but nothing in it says which display coordinate that was, and a summary reading the folder months later cannot recover it. The same point is marked **on screen** for ~2 s by the minigun's red aiming reticle at half its aiming size (`ScreenCaptureFlash.markCursor` → `EmojiAnimator.makeSniperReticle`), at **80 % opacity from the first frame** with the only fade in its last quarter: a mark that fades *in* asks to be watched arriving, and this one is already there when you look. It **zooms out** as it lands — in at 1.3×, settling to 0.9× over 0.35 s — so it reads as a scope being brought down onto the spot, and what stays behind is the smaller, quieter mark rather than the loud one that arrived. The border says what was captured; this says where you were pointing while the transcript recorded what you were saying about it. NB the anchored filename regex in the training-summarizer's `screenshot_index.py` had to learn about trailing detail (`_at…`, and the `-2` uniqueness suffix) — anchored on `\.jpg$` it silently dropped exactly those files. The timestamped name is what lets the `training-summarizer` / `call-summary` skills line each shot up against the transcript's `[HH:MM]` stamps and rank them by **dwell** (minutes until the next shot × words actually spoken over it, `screenshot_index.py` in the training-summarizer skill); anything a summary keeps is **copied into the session folder** by the skill that keeps it, so a cache wipe can never break a published note. `ScreenshotManager.sessionFolder` survives for `SessionNotesAppender` only — screenshots no longer go there
