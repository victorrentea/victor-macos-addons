# Whisper Transcription & Transcript Pickers

Live transcription (power-driven), its watchdogs, the ⌘⌃V "Ce tocmai am spus" picker, and the Python whisper-transcribe engine. The aggressive 😶😶😶 silent-transcription warning is documented in `displays-projector.md` (it is presentation-gated).

## 💬 Transcribing (feature)

- **💬 Transcribing** — Whisper live transcription runs **automatically whenever the Mac is on AC power, and pauses on battery**. There is no schedule, no workday window, and **no manual start/stop** — the only input that matters is the power source. The single, tiny `TranscriptionController` owns this: on AC it starts Whisper (if not already running) and a **60s heartbeat** restarts it if the process died (crash/OOM) while still plugged in; on battery it stops Whisper. On AC→battery it shows a "paused on battery" banner; on battery→AC a "resumed on AC" banner and Whisper restarts automatically (unlike the old model, AC restoration **does** auto-resume). It captures **two channels at once** (`whisper_runner.py`): your mic (the "selected device", auto-picked by priority Wireless Mic → Room Speakerphone → XLR → Bose → MacBook, overridable in the menu submenu) and the **audience from the `From Zoom` loopback**. **Lines carry a speaker glyph again since 2026-08-28 — `[HH:MM] 🎙️ text` for Victor, `👥` for the room, and no glyph at all when the classifier will not commit.** They were unlabelled between 2026-08-14 and then, and the reason for the removal is the reason the glyph could come back: see *Speaker identification* below. The two channels never separated two speakers — the room's audio and the mic pick each other up, so both channels hear *everyone* and whichever happened to be louder on a given chunk decided whether a sentence was filed under `Victor 🎙️:` or `Audience:`. A label that is wrong half the time is worse than no label: it invites every downstream reader (the summarizer, the ⌘⌃V picker, Victor re-reading his own day) to attribute words to the wrong mouth. The channels stay separate for *capture* — two devices, two threads, and the audience-side echo dedup that drops a near-duplicate of what the mic channel just wrote — they simply no longer claim to know who spoke. The per-line mic glyph (`Victor 🎙️:`, `Victor 💻:`) went with it; the `--- Victor → 💻 ---` device-switch markers stay, since they are how a source change is still recoverable. The **`[HH:MM] ` stamp is load-bearing** and must never be dropped: `TranscriptActivity` counts only stamped lines as speech, which is what keeps the whisper watchdog from reading a device marker as somebody talking. The menu item is a **read-only status row** (💬 Transcribing / Off – On Battery / Transcribing (off) while momentarily down) that opens the **mic-source picker submenu** — no Start/Stop, no ⌘⌃T hotkey, no 🔒 lock. The menu-bar icon is the live status: source emoji while running, leaf on battery, blinking red stop icon when on AC but Whisper isn't running (an error worth flagging, at any hour). The built-in mic's glyph is the **Apple logo** (U+F8FF, the bitten apple — "this Mac", not "a laptop"), not 💻: `MenuBarManager.glyphOverrides` swaps only what is *drawn*, because 💻 is the **wire value** whisper emits (`_ME_PATTERNS`) and every lookup — `availableSources`, the submenu checkmark — is string equality on it. It is also the only *monochrome* source glyph, so it is drawn with an explicit ink colour resolved from the surface's `effectiveAppearance` (white on dark, black on light) and re-baked on `AppleInterfaceThemeChangedNotification`; menu-item *titles* need no tint (AppKit draws them in the menu's own label colour). It is drawn **×1.25 larger** than an emoji at the same point size (`monochromeGlyphScale`): a text glyph sits inside its em-box and the apple fills far less of it — measured in an 18×18 icon, 💻 inks the full 18×18 while  inks only 10.5×13, which simply reads as a smaller icon. ×1.25 gets it to 13×16 and still clears both boxes we draw into (18×18 menu bar, 16×16 menu item); ×1.4 clips. The system font carries U+F8FF directly — "Apple Symbols", despite the name, does **not** (verified with `CTFontGetGlyphsForCharacters`; it only rendered through fallback).

  **Alive ≠ working — the output watchdog.** The 60 s heartbeat used to check only that the process existed, which is not the same as it doing anything: a capture thread inside whisper can die on its own while the process, its other thread and the PID all stay healthy, so the icon reads 💬 and not one word gets transcribed for hours (observed 2026-07-28: 4 h of nothing). The heartbeat therefore also watches the **output** — when whisper is alive on AC but no *speech* has landed for 5 min it is killed and restarted (`TranscriptionController.shouldForceRestart`, pure + unit-tested), gated by a 5 min warm-up since the last start (a fresh whisper is silent while it loads its model) and a 10 min floor between forced restarts (if a restart didn't fix it, looping is worse than the silence). "Speech" is `TranscriptActivity`, not the file's mtime: whisper writes `--- Victor → 💻 ---` device markers into the same transcript, and each one bumped the mtime and so granted another full staleness window — which is exactly why the 😶😶😶 warning kept clearing itself while nothing was being transcribed. Only `[HH:MM] Speaker: text` lines count, and `TranscriptionWatcher` now uses the same probe. **Never interpolate the silence value directly**: it is `.infinity` when nothing has been transcribed all day — the likeliest case when the watchdog fires — and `Int(.infinity)` *traps*, which crashed the whole app the first two times this triggered (2026-07-29, `EXC_BREAKPOINT` in `heartbeatTick`), before the restart it was announcing. Go through `TranscriptionController.describe`.

## 🎙️ "Ce tocmai am spus" picker (⌘⌃V)

- **🎙️ "Ce tocmai am spus" picker (⌘⌃V)** — **the last 40 seconds of transcript, distilled into five things worth pasting**; click one (or press its digit) and it goes to the clipboard. Replaced the *Emotional 🥹 Paste* that used to own this key (capture the clipboard on ⌘V, re-clean it on ⌘⌃V via Haiku): both answer "give me a tidy version of that", but the old one could only act on text already written down somewhere, and what gets lost mid-workshop is the sentence just *said out loud*.
    - **It compresses; it does not transcribe.** The first build cleaned the minute up *verbatim* and offered it at growing lengths, and that was the wrong product — tidied speech is still speech, and nobody wants a paragraph of their own hesitations on the clipboard. What is actually being reached for is the **idea**: a point of view, a line worth quoting, the bit that landed, or an instruction ready to hand to an agent. So the five slots are five **kinds** rather than five lengths: **1** the point of view in one line · **2** the idea in one or two sentences · **3** the funny line · **4** the emotional anchor · **5** an agent-ready imperative prompt — the four things Victor said he actually reaches for, plus the idea as the workhorse. A wider draft had two more and both were dropped rather than shuffled: **"everything, dense"** stopped being a distinct answer once options were capped at two lines (at 180 characters it converges on "the idea", and the dedup filter was catching them as one row), and **"title / hook"** was a guess nobody asked for — a guess does not earn a permanent slot on a panel scanned under time pressure. The captions under each row are **fixed in Swift** (`TranscriptDistiller.kinds`), never asked of the model: the shape is identical every run, so nothing can be hallucinated and the hand learns "the agent prompt is the last one" and stops reading. **The chrome is English, the options are not** — a caption is UI and matches every other label this app draws, while an option is *content* and comes back in whatever language was spoken. The panel is headed `🎙️ Recently spoke…` rather than naming the window: the number is an implementation detail that would go stale the moment it is retuned, and nobody reading this is checking a duration. **Every option is capped at two lines** (`maxCharsPerOption` = 180) — the panel is *scanned*, and rows each needing a paragraph of attention are not a menu, they are homework. That number is measured and set deliberately low: two lines of system text hold ~223 characters at the panel's text column, and the model treats a stated limit as a target and lands ~15 % over it, so asking for 180 lands near 205 and fits. The whole panel — box, padding and type alike — is drawn at **`scale` = 1.3**, because at 1× the options were legible rather than *glanceable* and this is read at a sitting distance while something else has your attention. Scaling **uniformly** is what keeps that character budget honest: the text column and the font grow by the same factor, so characters-per-line is unchanged and 180 still means two lines — growing the type alone would silently turn every two-line option into three. The one thing that can break it is a display narrow enough to clamp the width while the font keeps its size, which is why the screen cap is a generous 85 % of the visible frame. Slot 3 is told **never to invent a joke** that was not there — the sharpest phrasing instead — because a manufactured punchline is the one thing in this list you would paste and regret.
    - **40 seconds of speech, counted in lines (`TranscriptTail.lastSeconds`).** It was a full minute, and 40 s is better precisely because the options are *distillations*, not excerpts: over a minute the model has to average two or three separate thoughts into one "point of view" and the answer comes out true but bland, where 40 s is usually one thought — and one thought is what compresses into a line worth pasting. The window is measured in **lines, not clock time**, for the simple reason that `[HH:MM]` stamps cannot express 40 s at all; whisper emits one line per transcribed chunk, so the window is a line count. It was 8 lines while a chunk was a fixed 6 s minus 1 s of overlap; since chunks went to **(12, 2)** with an early flush on silence a line is worth more speech and no longer a fixed amount, so `secondsPerLine` is now an estimate (8 s) rather than arithmetic, and 40 s is the last 5 lines. It is deliberately biased **low**, because the number divides the window: under-estimating keeps *more* lines, and reaching slightly too far back is recoverable where stopping one sentence short is the failure this feature exists to avoid. That also makes it **speech**-time rather than wall-clock — silence writes no line, so the window is the last 40 seconds somebody was actually *talking*, not 40 seconds that were mostly nobody. A `maxMinutesBack` = 2 guard stops it reaching back across a coffee break, where eight lines of speech could otherwise span half an hour.
    - **The wait is the feature (`TranscriptSettlePolicy`).** At the instant the key is pressed, the words being reached for are **not in the file yet**: `whisper_runner` buffers a chunk, queues it, and only then runs inference, so the last sentence lands seconds later. Firing immediately produces a tidy answer that stops one sentence short — the one failure mode this cannot survive, because *it looks like it worked*. So the run waits on two clauses: a **floor** of 8 s (without it a keypress during a natural pause sees an already-quiet file and declares victory early). That 8 s used to be justified as *longer than a whole chunk plus inference*, which stopped being true when chunks went to 12 s — it is the **silence flush** that keeps the floor honest now: a keypress comes after a sentence, the pause flushes the buffer in 0.6 s, and the words are through the model well inside 8 s. Without the flush this number would have had to grow to ~18 s and the whole press to ~27 s **and then** 2.5 s of the transcript **not growing** (whisper writes a line per transcribed chunk, so continuous growth means the backlog still holds the newest words). `maxWait` 25 s gives up and says so on the panel — a picker that opens slightly stale beats one that never opens. Pure + unit-tested. End to end a press costs ~16–20 s: ~8 s of waiting, ~7–13 s of model, ~1 s of confirmation flash.
    - **The spinner rides the cursor (`BusyCursorSpinner`).** Ten silent seconds after a keypress is indistinguishable from an unbound key, and the natural next move is to press again. A real busy *cursor* is not available — `NSCursor` only applies over our own window and the whole point is that this fires over somebody else's app — so it is a 20 pt click-through panel chasing `NSEvent.mouseLocation` every 30 ms, parked below-right of the hotspot where cursor artwork leaves the quadrant free.
    - **`claude -p --model sonnet` on the subscription (`TranscriptDistiller`)**, the same auth path as `summarize-on-break.sh` / `flux-agent.sh` — because both Anthropic keys in `~/.training-assistants-secrets.env` answer *"credit balance is too low"* (re-verified 2026-08-14), which is why the emotional paste had quietly not worked for who knows how long: a shortcut whose failure output is "nothing happened" is indistinguishable from one that is merely slow. It is also why every launcher here starts with `env -u ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` — exported, the dead key shadows the subscription and fails auth. **Sonnet, not Haiku, and the reason is counterintuitive enough to write down: measured on the same minute, sonnet took 13 s and haiku 17 s.** The cost here is dominated by *output* tokens, and the weaker model spends more of them saying the same thing — so the better model was also the faster one. (`gemma3:4b` on the local Ollama does it in 8 s but drifts into third-person analysis — *"The speaker's proposed solution…"* — which is exactly wrong for text you are about to paste as your own. It is what the verbatim-cleanup build used, and dropping verbatim is what made a real model worth the seconds.) Override with `TRANSCRIPT_DISTILL_MODEL`. The binary path is **hardcoded** to `~/.local/bin/claude`: a GUI app launched by LaunchServices inherits almost no PATH, so the `command -v` the shell scripts can afford would fail here. Language is pinned to *the transcript's*, not the prompt's — sonnet answered an English minute in Romanian until told explicitly.
    - **Old transcripts are normalized on read.** Everything written before 2026-08-14 carries `Victor 🎙️:` / `Audience:` prefixes; left in, they travel through the model and out onto the clipboard as "Victor: Da!", which is filing metadata, not something anybody said. `TranscriptTail.stripSpeaker` takes them off, matched against the **two labels whisper could ever write** rather than a general `^word:` rule — speech genuinely contains colons ("regula e asta:") and eating a real clause to tidy a legacy prefix is the worse trade.
    - **The pick flashes for a second before the panel goes (`TranscriptPicker.flashChosen`).** Pressing a digit is a blind gesture: the eye is on the row it wants, the finger is on a number, and nothing on screen connects the two — so without this the panel simply vanishes and you are left trusting that the digit you pressed was the row you meant. The chosen row rises to a bright amber in 0.12 s and decays back over the remaining second while the others fade to 25 %; the **asymmetry is what makes it read as a flash** rather than a fade, arriving at once and leaving slowly, so it is still glowing as the panel closes under it. The clipboard is written **at the press, not after the animation** — a second is exactly long enough that a fast hand would otherwise ⌘V an empty answer — and the panel stops accepting picks for the duration (except Esc), since a second press would copy something other than what is visibly lit.
    - **A window with nothing in it never reaches the model.** Under `minWordsWorthDistilling` = 10 words the run stops with a Basso and says how few there were. Found live: pressed after a lull, the 40 s window came down to the single word "Da!", and sonnet answered — quite reasonably — with a sentence explaining there was nothing to distill, which the parser could only report as a failure. Twelve seconds and an error to be told what a word count already knew. The prompt was also told to return 7 options regardless and never to answer with an explanation, and a reply that still comes back as prose is now **quoted into the error**: when the model refuses it is *saying why*, and swallowing that turns a legible refusal into a mystery in the log.
    - It **only ever writes the clipboard**, never simulates the paste: by the time the panel has been read and clicked, the app that had focus at keypress may not be where the text should go, and a shortcut that types a paragraph into the wrong window is worse than one that makes you press ⌘V. The panel opens centred on the screen **under the cursor** (not the built-in Retina, which a venue projector mirrors), takes focus so the digit keys work — an accessory app gets no keystrokes otherwise — and `NSApp.deactivate()`s on close so that ⌘V lands back where it was meant to. Clicking away or Esc dismisses; a second ⌘⌃V while a run is in flight is a no-op (that silent wait is exactly what invites an impatient second press). Test hook: `GET /test/transcript-picker?at=HH:MM` (today) or `?at=2026-08-14%2019:18` (any past session), which rewinds to that minute and skips the wait. Without it the feature can only be exercised while somebody is actually talking — and it needs testing precisely when it is least testable: after hours, on battery, past midnight, when today's file is empty or does not exist. Every recorded session is a corpus of real, messy Whisper output. **The wait itself is logged** (`waited 8.1s for whisper (caught up)`) because it is the one part of this that looks identical working and broken, right up until the newest sentence is missing from the options.

**Operational note (2026-06):** Transcription control was simplified to a single power-driven rule (on AC → run, on battery → pause) in `TranscriptionController`. The former `TranscriptionStateMachine` (off/on/onWorkday/battery + persisted UserDefaults state), `TranscriptionScheduler` (Mon–Fri 09:00–18:00 window + hard lock), the 18:00 `TranscriptionCountdownOverlay`, the ⌘⌃T toggle, and the menu Start/Stop were all removed. There is no persisted on/off state anymore; the launch UI starts not-running and the controller reflects the real process/power state.

## whisper-transcribe
Live dual-channel Whisper transcription engine (extracted from training-assistant daemon):
- **WhisperTranscriptionRunner** — captures audio from 2 sources, transcribes via mlx-whisper on Apple Silicon GPU

**Chunking: (12 s, 2 s overlap), flushed early on silence (2026-08-26).** It was (6, 1), and the reason to change it is that **mlx-whisper pads every input to a fixed 30 s mel window** — the encoder costs the same for 2 s of audio as for 15 s. Measured over four hours of a real workshop recording on this M1 Max: 0.79 s at 2 s of audio, 1.00 s at 15 s, i.e. 7.5× the audio for 1.27× the compute. Short chunks were paying for a 30 s encoder pass to transcribe five seconds. (12, 2) halves the calls per minute — 12 down to 6 — for **49.7 % less compute**, and the wider overlap comes free in the same trade: nothing merges the overlap at the text level, so re-transcribed audio surfaces as duplicated words at boundaries, but doubling the overlap while halving the number of boundaries wins monotonically — 12.8 % duplication at (6,1), 8.3 % at (8,1.5), **6.5 % at (12,2)**, 5.5 % at (15,2).

What does *not* follow, and was believed briefly on synthetic audio: that long chunks avoid whisper's repetition-hallucination loops (a chunk decoding `"Ter Ter Ter…"` at 5–10× a normal call's cost). On real audio those fire on **true silence** (RMS < 0.001) at every length; the per-device RMS gate in `_cb` already screens that case. Fewer calls still means less exposure, but the mechanism is silence, not brevity.

**The silence flush is what makes 12 s chunks safe.** A fixed 12 s buffer means the last sentence spoken can sit unemitted for a full chunk — double the old tail latency, and `TranscriptSettlePolicy.minWait` would have had to go from 8 s to ~18 s, turning ⌘⌃V from a ~16 s wait into a ~27 s one. So `_cb` also emits when the room goes quiet: `_SILENCE_FLUSH_SEC` (0.6 s) of below-threshold blocks with at least `_MIN_FLUSH_SEC` (1.5 s) of **speech** in the buffer. Two details are load-bearing and both are unit-tested (`tests/test_silence_flush.py`). The 1.5 s is measured against the *speech* in the buffer, not the buffer — trailing silence accumulates in `_buf` too, so counting it meant a half-word plus a long enough pause cleared the bar on silence alone and emitted a fragment. And a flushed chunk carries **no overlap** into the next: the cut is a pause, no word straddles it, and re-transcribing the tail would only duplicate text. The side benefit matters downstream — a flushed chunk tends to hold one utterance by one speaker, which is exactly the shape speaker identification wants.
- Writes `[HH:MM] text` lines to per-day files in `TRANSCRIPTION_FOLDER` — **no speaker prefix** (see the 💬 Transcribing entry for why both channels stopped claiming to know who spoke)
- Default devices: XLR mic (device 5) + Zoom loopback (device 17)
- Auto-detects Romanian/English, filters hallucinations, RMS-based silence skipping
- Queue-aware batching merges adjacent same-speaker chunks before inference; default wait budget is tuned for non-live subtitle use
- Adaptive quality *can* switch to a faster Whisper model when the backlog grows and return to balanced when the queue drains — but **it is a no-op today**: `WHISPER_MODEL_FAST` is set nowhere (not in the repo, the Swift launcher, or the plist), so it defaults to `_MODEL_BALANCED`, and `_select_model_mode()` short-circuits to `"balanced"` on exactly that equality. Under load it buys nothing. Either point it at a real smaller model or delete the path
- Startup guard: `WHISPER_PARENT_PID` is mandatory; runner exits early if missing/invalid (prevents unmanaged launches)
- Parent sentinel thread exits whisper when Swift parent dies; combined with Swift-side pre-start cleanup to reduce stale/orphan runners during automated app restarts

**PortAudio discipline (2026-07) — one root cause behind both the crashes and the silence.** `_portaudio_reinit()` is `Pa_Terminate()`: **process-global**, it closes *both* channels' live streams. Three rules keep it from tearing the app apart, all learned the hard way:
1. **Only reinit when the device set actually changed** (`_device_set_changed()`, read from CoreAudio — asking PortAudio would need the very reinit we're avoiding; errors answer "yes", since a needless reinit is survivable and missing a hot-plugged mic is not). `_check_best_device` used to call it unconditionally, so *every* device check killed two healthy streams.
2. **Never touch PortAudio on CoreAudio's notification thread.** The ctypes listeners invoke the handler directly, so `_on_device_list_changed` now just sets an `Event` and a `device-check` worker does the work — it also **coalesces**, because one dock/projector plug fires the listener once per input device and each of those used to spawn its own teardown. The old code slept 2 s and then called `Pa_Terminate()` right there while CoreAudio held its locks; the crash reports show exactly that, as a double-free on the `HALC_ProxyNotification Call Listener Queue`.
3. **`self._pref_watch_mtime` is seeded from the file's real mtime**, not `0.0`. Starting at `0.0` meant the preference watcher's first poll *always* reported a phantom change — a fake source switch ~1 s into **every single run**, which detonated PortAudio underneath two streams that had just opened. That one line produced the paired `Invalid stream pointer [PaErrorCode -9988]` on every start (85 in one day's log) and the SIGABRT double-frees.

**Threads must never die quietly.** A capture thread that dies is invisible from outside: the process lives, the *other* channel keeps working, the PID is healthy, the icon says 💬 — and nothing is transcribed. That happened because `_loop`'s error handler called `self._resolve_fn()` **unguarded inside an `except` block**; an exception there is not caught by its own `try`, so it escaped and killed the thread. Only the Victor channel has a `_resolve_fn`, which is why only Victor's thread ever died. Now: that call is wrapped, both `_loop` and `_transcriber_loop` run under `_supervised_*` wrappers that log and restart on anything (including `BaseException`), threads are **named**, and a `threading.excepthook` routes any remaining escape into our own log.

**Quiet crashes (no "Python quit unexpectedly" dialog).** The interpreter is `Python.app` — a bundled GUI app — so a fatal signal used to put a modal crash alert on the projector mid-talk. `_install_quiet_death_handlers` installs libc's `_exit` as the C-level SIGABRT/SIGSEGV/SIGBUS handler (async-signal-safe; a Python handler would deadlock, since SIGABRT from libmalloc arrives holding the malloc lock), so the process dies by *status* instead of by uncaught signal: no crash report, no dialog. **The trade-off is real — we lose the `.ips` file.** `faulthandler` cannot rescue it: it refuses to `register()` fatal signals ("use `enable()` instead") and `enable()` re-raises with the DEFAULT action rather than chaining, which is the dialog again. What replaces it: everything Python logged on the way down, the thread hook above, and `WhisperProcessManager`'s termination handler, which now decodes the status (`6` = SIGABRT/likely PortAudio double-free, `10` = SIGBUS, `11` = SIGSEGV) into an `overlayError`. Set `WHISPER_QUIET_CRASH=0` to get crash reports back. SIGTERM/SIGINT also get a handler that stops the runner and `os._exit(0)`s, skipping CPython's shutdown — which is where `atexit` would otherwise tear PortAudio down with callbacks still live. (The parent sentinel has always used `os._exit`, which is why it never crashed.)

**Whisper's stderr is decoded incrementally.** `WhisperProcessManager` used to drop the **entire** pipe buffer when `String(data:encoding:.utf8)` returned nil — and whisper's output is emoji-dense, so a chunk regularly ends mid-codepoint. That is how a Python traceback explaining a dead capture thread could vanish without a trace. Partial tails now carry over to the next read and no buffer is ever discarded.

## 🔴 Raw audio capture (`WHISPER_RECORD_RAW`)

**Off by default, armed per session** — it writes ~115 MB an hour and records a room full of people who were not asked. The menu row (`🔴 Recording raw audio — 2.4 h`, at the bottom of the Transcribing submenu, with the 🔴 echoed on the parent row) exists precisely because that state is otherwise invisible, and a state you cannot see is one you forget to turn off.

**Why it exists.** The speaker-identification work has no far-field corpus. Every recording that survived is a Zoom mix of *remote* participants, where Zoom's VTT is reliable ground truth; the case that actually matters — an audience **in the room**, arriving through Victor's own lavalier — was never captured. A calendar sweep back to 2021 found 40+ on-site sessions and only three hybrid ones — two in early 2024, one in April 2026 — and **none of them was recorded**. So it has to be collected going forward.

- **The state is a file, not a defaults key**: `addons-output/.record-raw-audio` (`RawAudioRecording`). It has to be settable from *outside* the app — whisper is a separate process that only learns of it through its environment, and a scheduled job needs to arm it before a workshop with nobody clicking anything. Whisper reads it **at launch**, so toggling bounces the process (the same stop → 1.5 s → start the watchdog uses, for the beat PortAudio needs to release the devices).
- **Recorded before the RMS gate**, in `_cb`. The corpus needs the silence and the below-threshold audio too, because the gate is one of the things this work may want to change — baking it in would make the corpus unable to answer the question it was collected for.
- **Headerless PCM, appended** — `raw-audio/<day>-<label>.s16le16k.pcm`. Not a WAV, and that is not laziness: Python's `wave` has no append mode, and a WAV writes its frame count only on `close()`, while this process is routinely killed outright (the quiet-crash handlers `_exit`, the watchdog force-restarts). A killed run would leave a file declaring itself empty. Raw PCM costs at most the last block. Read it with `ffmpeg -f s16le -ar 16000 -ac 1 -i <file> out.wav`. Appending also matters because the heartbeat restarts whisper several times a day, and a file per run would shatter the day into fragments that no longer line up with the transcript's clock.
- **It must never touch the live transcription.** `write()` is called from PortAudio's realtime callback, so it does a non-blocking put onto a bounded queue (`_RECORD_QUEUE_BLOCKS`, 600 ≈ 60 s of slack) that a writer thread drains; a full queue **drops audio and counts it**, and `write()` swallows *everything*, including `BaseException`. Losing a second of corpus is nothing; stuttering the transcript in front of a room is not. A recorder that fails to start is logged and set to `None` — whisper carries on.
- **Arming it for a morning nobody will be at the laptop**: `arm-raw-recording.sh` + a one-shot LaunchAgent. It writes the flag and then kills whisper, because whisper reads `WHISPER_RECORD_RAW` at launch and the flag alone changes nothing — the 60 s heartbeat in `TranscriptionController` brings the process back with recording on, so no other component has to know the job exists. On battery there is no whisper to kill and the flag simply waits for AC. It unloads itself and deletes its own plist after firing: a job that quietly returns a year later to record a room is what this feature is careful not to be.
- `WHISPER_RECORD_RAW=all` also records the loopback channel, which in a hybrid room is clean ground truth for the remote speakers (the mic is not).

## 🗣️ Speaker identification (`WHISPER_SPEAKER_ID`) — dark-launched

**On by default, and in the transcript, since 2026-08-28. The scores still go only to `<day>-speakers.jsonl`.** It was opt-in behind `WHISPER_SPEAKER_ID` for its first two days and nothing ever set the variable, so it never ran once — the room day of 2026-08-27 produced no verdict file at all and its calibration had to be reconstructed offline from the raw PCM. A dark launch that is dark to itself collects nothing.

**The glyph in the transcript is a different claim from the one removed on 2026-08-14.** The old labels came from *which input device was louder* on a 12 s chunk; in a room both channels hear everyone, so that was a coin flip with a microphone attached. What is written now is an enrolled voiceprint, one cosine per *whisper segment*, thresholds refitted on a full day of real room audio and checked against 62 clips Victor labelled by ear — 81 % of his segments labelled, 4.8 % mislabelled, every audience segment on the right side, and the middle band left deliberately blank. Two invariants make it safe to write into a file people read:

- **No word is ever lost to a label.** Transcript lines are written from whisper's own segments, and `_score_speakers` refuses to split a chunk whose segment texts do not join back into the chunk text — a capped list, a dropped piece, anything — in which case the chunk goes out whole and unattributed. A missing sentence is far worse than a missing label. An unscorable segment (too short, hallucinated) still gets its words written; it just gets no glyph.
- **The `[HH:MM] ` stamp stays first.** `TranscriptActivity` reads characters 0/3/6 to decide a line is speech, and `TranscriptDistiller.speechWordCount` strips the stamp — and now the glyph too, since counting it would re-introduce the per-line inflation that stripping exists to prevent.

An abstention is written as a plain unlabelled line, not as a third glyph, because that is exactly what it means: nobody is claiming anything about this line. Labels were in the transcript once (`Victor 🎙️:` / `Audience:`, from whichever input device was louder) and were removed on 2026-08-14 for being wrong often enough to poison every downstream reader. Putting them back is a claim to be *proven* against a day of real workshop audio, not a change to be trusted on review — so the wiring is deliberately incapable of writing a label into the transcript, and unit-tested to stay that way.

**It is verification, not diarization.** Diarization asks "how many voices, and who spoke when": clustering, global state, and the right to relabel the past. The question here is narrower and far cheaper — one enrolled voiceprint, one cosine per segment, no state, no retroactive edits.

**Calibration, measured on Victor's own voice (2026-08-27).** Enrolled from 151 segments (17.1 min) of one recorded session; scored against 217 of his own segments and 620 impostor segments from *other* sessions 6 and 12 months away. Held-out separation is wide — his mean cosine 0.706 (p5 0.500) against an impostor mean of 0.135 (p95 0.293) — but the tail is what sets the thresholds:

| errors/side | `audience ≤` | `victor ≥` | his speech mislabelled | impostor mislabelled | **abstains on his speech** | abstains overall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.0 % | 0.333 | 0.506 | 2.30 % | 2.10 % | 3.2 % | 2.4 % |
| **1.5 % (shipped)** | **0.321** | **0.643** | **1.84 %** | **1.61 %** | **17.5 %** | **6.5 %** |
| 1.0 % | 0.316 | 0.666 | 1.38 % | 1.13 % | 26.3 % | 9.1 % |
| 0.5 % | 0.230 | 0.707 | 0.92 % | 0.65 % | 38.7 % | 19.7 % |

The knee is sharp: buying half a point of accuracy past 1.5 % costs ten points of coverage, because a single colliding voice sets the upper threshold almost by itself. **One participant accounts for 6 of the 9 false accepts in the whole corpus, peaking at 0.752 — above Victor's own 5th percentile of 0.462.** All nine come from a single session, the only one with several Romanian male voices; 21 of the 32 speakers never exceed 0.334. If this becomes a problem the fix is to enrol a known collider as a second speaker and take the nearest, not to raise the threshold on everyone.

1.5 % was shipped because the governing rule here is *a wrong label is worse than no label* — the reason the old labels were removed. **Those numbers were from remote Zoom audio, and the room moved them a long way.**

**Room calibration, and what it cost to get it (2026-08-27/28).** The first far-field day was captured on 2026-08-27 — 6h53m through the XLR lavalier, 08:05→21:13 — and replayed through the live chunking offline. The Zoom thresholds abstained on **27.9 %** of it against 6.5 % on Zoom, so the obvious reading was that the room is a harder channel. It is the opposite. Enrolling a second voiceprint *from the room itself* (720 segments, 87 min) produced a print with a **0.930** cosine to the Zoom one and no better separation at all (his 10th percentile 0.34 → 0.36, the audience's maximum 0.16 → 0.17): the room is not a different channel and the second print is not worth its complexity. What actually changed is the *impostor*. On Zoom, the other speakers had their own microphones and sounded like themselves, one of them reaching 0.752; in a room they arrive across ten metres of air into a lavalier clipped 20 cm from Victor's mouth, and far-field degradation costs them far more than it costs him. **In the room the audience tops out at 0.163 while his own 10th percentile is 0.34** — the two populations separate around 0.21, not 0.64.

The verdicts came from Victor labelling **62 blind clips by ear** (a listening page, stratified across the score range, no scores or bands shown until after each answer): 38 him, **15 both voices in one chunk**, 5 somebody else, 4 unsure. Reweighted to the day: **~79 % him, ~17 % mixed, ~1.8 % pure audience.**

That last number is the finding. The audience almost never *owns* a chunk — they are either below the RMS gate entirely or inside twelve seconds that Victor dominates — and so no threshold can make an `Audience` verdict on a chunk trustworthy: at its best it was 45 % correct, on under 5 % of the day. **The mixed chunk, not the threshold, was the error.** See *One verdict per whisper segment* below.

Shipped thresholds are now **`victor_at` 0.45 / `audience_below` 0.20**, measured on whisper segments of at least 2 s: 81 % of his segments labelled, 4.8 % mislabelled, and every one of the audience's segments below the floor. **The audience side rests on 5 clips and is the weakest number in this document** — it is written down here precisely so the next day of audio can contradict it. The Zoom numbers are kept in the voiceprint's metadata (`zoom_victor_at`, `zoom_audience_below`) rather than deleted.

- **One verdict per whisper segment, not per chunk** (`_score_speakers`). This is the diarisation step, and it is small because the hard part had already been solved twice by someone else: whisper returns segments cut at the pauses in speech — which is where a speaker change happens — and there is an enrolled reference, so the question is never "how many voices and who" but only "is this stretch him". Scoring the whole chunk was the bug: a 12 s chunk in a room routinely holds a question from the floor with Victor's answer over the top, and one cosine over the whole thing averages the two into a number in the middle. That band never meant "unsure". It meant "you asked about two people at once". Measured against the 62 ear-labelled clips, segment-level scoring puts a high and a low verdict on the same chunk in **60 %** of the mixed ones and on **0 %** of the ones that were only ever him — the false-alarm rate being the number that matters, since an invented split attributes a sentence to a stranger. Cost is one embedding per segment instead of one per chunk, ~2–3× of 25 ms. A chunk whisper did not segment usefully still gets exactly one whole-chunk verdict, written **without** `start`/`end`, so the absence of those fields says "this could not be cut finer".
- **A hallucinated segment gets no verdict at all.** `_is_garbage` already screened the chunk; it now screens each segment too. A hallucinated line has no speaker, and that is different from an uncertain one — two of the three worst errors in the labelled set were whisper writing *"Să vă mulțumim pentru a avea"* over a cough and the scorer dutifully calling the cough somebody else.
- **A second duration floor, `SEGMENT_MIN_SECONDS` (2.0 s).** `MIN_SECONDS` (3.0 s) was fitted on whole chunks; a segment cut at a pause is one person talking rather than an average over twelve seconds, and holds up further down. At a 1.0 s floor his tail falls into the audience's territory (10th percentile 0.26 → 0.22), which is why the floor is 2.0 and not lower. The floor belongs to the population being scored, so it is a parameter of `decide`, not a second module constant read inside it.
- **Three answers, not two** (`speaker_id.decide`, pure + unit-tested). Two thresholds carve out a band in the middle where the honest answer is *nothing*. A single threshold would force a guess on every segment, which is precisely how the old labels earned their removal. Abstaining is a correct outcome, not a failure.
- **Calibrated on cross-session data.** Enrolment comes from one session; the thresholds are fitted against segments recorded 6–12 months later, on a possibly different mic, because that is the condition this runs in — enrolled once, used for years. The same-session number flatters and is not used. `enroll_voiceprint.py` **refuses to invent thresholds**; they are required arguments.
- **A degenerate embedding scores `NaN`, not `0.0`.** The tempting shortcut is fatal: `0.0` is an ordinary *low* score, and a low score means `Audience` — so "the measurement failed" would silently become "somebody else was speaking". `NaN` cannot be mistaken for a score, and `decide` abstains on it explicitly rather than letting it fall through the comparisons (all of which are `False` against `NaN`, which would give the right answer with an unreadable reason).
- **Nothing under 1.5 s of audio is scored at all.** Every model measured sits at 3–5 % EER below that — a 0.99 there is not evidence, it is the shape of a confidently wrong label. The duration gate is checked *before* the score, and before the model runs.
- **`wespeaker ResNet34-LM`, 25 MB ONNX, through raw onnxruntime.** Measured 25.2 ms per 6 s chunk = **6.7 % of whisper's 375 ms**, ~97 MB RSS, EER 0.93 % at 2 s clean / 1.85 % at 10 dB babble. Two traps: **`sherpa-onnx` 1.13.6 silently skips feature mean-normalisation for wespeaker models** — 15.5 % EER through sherpa versus 0.73 % through raw ORT with CMN, with no error anywhere — and **CoreML's fast path is the GPU**, which is exactly what whisper is holding (measured under contention: GPU path −79 %, CPU/ANE path −7.6 %), so the provider is pinned to `CPUAndNeuralEngine`. `intra_op_num_threads` is pinned to 4; onnxruntime's default of one-per-core measured slower and much noisier at p90.
- **The feature extractor is numpy, not torch.** The reference wespeaker recipe uses `torchaudio.compliance.kaldi.fbank`, but this process holds mlx-whisper on the GPU at ~1.8 GB and is restarted several times a day — torch would add hundreds of MB and seconds of import to buy something numpy can do (`kaldi_fbank.py`, verified against torchaudio to a cosine of >0.9999 on the resulting embeddings).
- **`ERes2NetV2` was evaluated and rejected**, despite being the paper favourite for short utterances: the only published checkpoint is Mandarin, and its between-speaker cosine floor is 0.28–0.37 against 0.05–0.09 for the English models. The paper was right about the architecture, not about what can be downloaded.
- **Every failure disables the feature and costs nothing.** A missing model, a corrupt voiceprint, an ONNX that no longer loads — all log one line and return `None`. `SpeakerScorer.available` exists because "scoring nothing" and "scoring and abstaining" produce identical empty output and are entirely different problems.
- **The voiceprint never enters the repo.** `victor-macos-addons` is **public**, and a speaker-verification template is precisely the artefact that defeats speaker verification. It lives at `addons-output/voiceprint.npz` (gitignored), stored as plain arrays with `allow_pickle=False`, carrying its own provenance — what it was enrolled from, when, how many minutes, and the self-similarity spread, because a voiceprint averaged over a contaminated set looks exactly like a good one until it is used.
